**Meiji Jingu (Tokyo)**

Meiji Jingu is Tokyo's most prominent Shinto shrine, set inside a large forested park in the middle of Harajuku. It's dedicated to Emperor Meiji and Empress Shoken — calm, green, and a real contrast to the busy city around it.

It's one of the top spots for hatsumode (first shrine visit of the New Year), drawing millions of people over the first few days of January.

&emsp;&emsp;**Best season/month**

- January 1–3 for hatsumode (very crowded but atmospheric).
- Any season for forest walks — the 70-hectare woodland is quiet year-round.

<img src="../images/meiji_forest_path.jpg" alt="Forested path through Meiji Jingu" style="width: 80%; height: auto;" />

&emsp;&emsp;**Practical note**

- Directly next to Harajuku Station — easy to combine with Takeshita Street or Yoyogi Park in the same visit.
- Free to enter the main grounds; the inner garden (Gyoen) has a small fee.
