**Tosho-gu (Nikko)**

Tosho-gu is the main shrine complex in Nikko, built to enshrine Tokugawa Ieyasu — the shogun who unified Japan. It's over-the-top ornate compared to most Japanese shrines: gold leaf, intricate carvings, painted gates. UNESCO World Heritage.

It's definitely more visually busy than minimalist Shinto architecture you'll find elsewhere, but that's exactly the point — it was built to impress.





&emsp;&emsp;**Best season/month**

- Spring (late April) and autumn (mid-November) for the combination of foliage and shrine color.
- Summer is lush and green but can be hot and crowded.



&emsp;&emsp;**Practical note**

- Entry to the main Yomei-mon gate area and inner shrine requires a separate ticket from the general grounds.
- Allow at least 2 hours for the full complex; combine with Rinno-ji temple and the surrounding mountain area.
- See [Nikko](../locations/regions/3.%20Kanto/Tochigi/Nikko.md) for day-trip logistics from Tokyo.
